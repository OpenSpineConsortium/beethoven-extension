These images were made on the 9.0.0 build and are unchanged for 10.0.0.

Made 2026-10-01 between 01:46 and 02:03 UTC for 9.0.0 and rebuilt once at 02:38 to 02:40 UTC (the stills
came out byte-identical; the video changed as described under promo/beethoven-promo.mp4), the release whose
pages wear the site's theme (the cream ground, ink type, the teal accent, Newsreader and IBM Plex), from

HOW THEY ARE MADE (src/)

  node src/build_assets.mjs --pw <dir of an installed playwright package> [--only shots|tiles|icon|video]
                            [--sync-site <the site repository>] [--webm]

build_assets.mjs opens each page in src/ in Playwright's Chromium at the image's exact size and device
scale 1 and takes a screenshot of it; it fails if a page makes any request that is not a local file, if a
font is not loaded, if the page is not exactly the image's size, or if a PNG is not 24-bit RGB without alpha.
The pages:
  store.css           the site's tokens (style.css), its headline, line and fine-print styles (hero.css), the
                      hero window's shadow, and the drafting grid behind the hero
  split.css           screenshots 2 to 4: the words left, the screen right
  shot-01..05.html, tile-440x280.html, marquee-1400x560.html, promo.html
  common.js           puts the diagram in, cuts each screen's card out of its capture (data-box, in the
                      capture's pixels), and waits for the fonts and images
  diagram.js, diagram.css
                      the home page diagram and its rules, copied verbatim from the site repository
                      (tools/pages/index/body.html between flow:start and flow:end; assets/css/hero.css);
                      --sync-site copies them again
  fonts/              Newsreader (the variable roman and italic, byte-identical to google/fonts main,
                      ofl/newsreader) and IBM Plex Sans and Mono (the Latin1 cuts the extension bundles), each
                      with its SIL Open Font License text. Nothing is fetched.
  icon/               the icon stream's work (its README says how to regenerate it)

The screens are the captures in raw-9/ (raw-9/LIST.txt and raw-9/capture-log.txt say how they were taken:
the 9.0.0 release build in Playwright Chromium against the mock portal of the store reviewer's recipe). Each
image shows one card exactly as captured: cut out of the capture along the card's own border, scaled, given
the card's own corner radii and the hero window's shadow. Nothing inside a card is drawn, edited or
composited. The video is drawn frame by frame: promo.html exposes __seek(t), which sets every animation on
the page to time t; each frame is a screenshot, and ffmpeg (found on PATH, else inside WSL) encodes them.
Without ffmpeg (or with --webm) the script records the page in real time with Playwright's recorder and
keeps a .webm instead (VP8, about 28.9 s with the page's loading at the start); YouTube takes either.

CAVEATS

- The strip is captured as a tab at the side panel's width (360 px), with the side panel switched off for
  the run; in Chrome a person sees the same page in the side panel beside the portal.
- The account and machine names in the screens are the mock portal's stand-ins (st1234, localhost,
  node01-amber-lantern, student@example.edu), not anyone's account.
- Google's image guidance suggests promo tiles with little text and without light backgrounds. These tiles
  follow the site's cream and carry the name and one line, as the brief asked.
- Google's image page says a store icon may have transparency (96x96 artwork in 16 px of transparent
  padding) and that one without an alpha channel is placed in a frame with rounded corners.
  store-icon-128.png has no alpha, as the brief asked; ../public/icon/128.png is the same mark with
  transparent padding, if the frame looks wrong on the listing.
- The motion in the video that is neither a capture nor the site's own animation: the diagram drawing
  itself, the scenes coming in and going, the button's press and the sign-in strip moving up.

THE REQUEST LOGS IN e2e/

Each lists every request the browser made, who made it, the vendor requests with their bodies and
answers, and every line typed into the portal's shell and what it printed. They are the record of the
8.0.0 and earlier runs; the 9.0.0 captures are described in raw-9/.
  run-I-approved-bound-st1234.txt  approved and bound, on the lab's shared copy: the mock reached at
      https://ondemand.wayne.localhost:8443 (the hostname makes the site "wayne") with --shared-install at the
      path profiles/wayne.json names and --shared-version 8, and services.json pointed at the mock's access
      stub (one installation per approved account). The check carried install_id; one install line, no
      framework file uploaded; Details "tool: the lab's shared copy, version 8"; online; seventeen checks OK.
      Afterwards the stub, asked directly, approved the same identifier, refused another with
      "other_installation" and refused a check without one with "update_required".
  run-J-second-installation-st1234.txt  a second installation: a fresh browser profile, the same mock user, the
      stub started with st1234 already bound to run I's identifier. The service answered "other_installation";
      the strip said "This account is already set up on another computer. Ask your lab lead to move it here."
      with no button (run-J-strip-other-installation.png is that strip, as evidence, not a store image);
      in the 75 s watched afterwards nothing was typed into the portal's shell and no job was submitted; the
      stub still approved run I's installation.
  run-K-no-install-id-update-required-st1234.txt  a client that sends no installation identifier (a build
      before 8.0.0), played with curl against the stub: "update_required"; with --install-optional (what the
      deployed service does while REQUIRE_INSTALL_ID is "0") it is approved, and a second identifier is still
      refused.
  run-L-store-screenshots-reviewer-recipe-st1234.txt  the store reviewer's recipe against the real access
      service: the source of the nine 8.0.0 screenshots; the check went to beethoven-access with product
      "bridge", version "8.0.0" and install_id; online; seventeen checks OK.
  run-M-real-service-one-installation-st1234.txt  the rule on the real service, with curl and admin.mjs: the
      reviewers' exemption on st1234 was switched off for the run, a fresh identifier was approved and bound,
      a second one was refused with "other_installation" (the maintainer's second-installation e-mail follows
      from that, at most once a day), and then the seat was reset (admin.mjs reset-seat st1234) and the
      exemption put back (admin.mjs seat-rule st1234 any), so the reviewers' recipe keeps working; two more
      fresh identifiers were then approved without binding.
  run-A to run-H  the 4.0.0 to 7.0.0 runs, kept as they were written: they are the record of those builds.

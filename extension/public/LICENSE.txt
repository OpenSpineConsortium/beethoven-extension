5. WHAT BEETHOVEN DOES WITH YOUR CREDENTIALS AND YOUR WORK

This section describes the software as shipped, so that you can hold Licensor to it.

(a) Claude. Beethoven never reads, stores, copies, prints or relays a Claude credential. You sign in
to Claude Code yourself, through Anthropic's own flow, once, in a terminal on a compute node of your
own cluster account. The Extension starts that terminal for you, in the same command that starts
your worker, through your portal's shell, opens Anthropic's sign-in page in a new tab, shows you one
box, and closes that tab once Claude Code reports you are signed in, unless the tab has loaded
another page since you pasted the code; you sign in on Anthropic's page and paste the one-time code
it shows into that box. The Extension types the code once into that terminal and keeps no copy of
it: it is not stored, not written to the audit log and not sent to Licensor or anyone else, and the
Extension never reads Anthropic's page or your clipboard to obtain it. Claude Code makes its
credential from the code and keeps it in its own folder in your cluster account; the Framework only
starts Claude Code where that credential already is, and Beethoven reads Claude Code's own report of
whether it is signed in, with the account and plan that report names, nothing more. You use one
Claude account, your own. You answer Claude Code's one-time question about Remote Control yourself,
with the box you tick in the Extension's panel before your first worker starts; the Framework
records that answer in one file in your cluster account when you press Connect, and your worker
gives it to Claude Code when Claude Code asks. Trust for the worker's own folder is recorded in
Claude Code's settings by the Framework when a worker starts. Beethoven gives Claude Code no answer
you have not given.

(b) Your sign-in to your cluster. The Extension reaches your cluster in one way: through your
institution's Open OnDemand portal, which may need your institution's VPN (at Wayne State,
GlobalProtect), in a browser session signed in to there. It signs in to your portal for you with the
account name and password you give it, or uses the sign-in you make yourself in your browser's own
sign-in box, as (i) says. It never signs in to Claude for you (see (a)). Versions 12.0.0 to 15.0.0
of the Extension also offered a direct way over ssh, through a helper program (the "Bridge")
installed on your computer, a sign-in sheet of the Extension's own and a key kept in your browser;
this version has no Bridge, no ssh connection of its own and no such key, and (ii) says what it
leaves alone. The sign-in sheet of (i) is not that way: it signs in to your portal only, over the
same connection as everything else the Extension does there, which still needs your institution's
VPN where it requires one, and it asks for no verification code.

(i) Your portal. The Extension makes its requests to your institution's Open OnDemand portal, on
that one website only, from its own background and without a portal tab where your browser allows
it, with the sign-in your browser holds for that website. It never reads that sign-in and never
touches a multi-factor step. When the portal asks for a sign-in, the Extension signs in for you with
the password you give it, or you sign in yourself in your browser's own sign-in box.

The Extension's own sign-in. Where your browser lets the Extension answer a website's request for a
sign-in, as Chrome does, it asks for your account name and your cluster password on its own sign-in
sheet, in its panel, whenever your portal wants a sign-in. By typing them there and pressing "Sign
in" you ask the Extension to sign in to your portal for you with them, and agree that it may. It
answers, through your browser and not with a page script, only the portal's request for a sign-in
that its own background reading of your portal's dashboard page receives, at most once each time it
checks whether you are signed in, and only when that request comes from your one portal website
itself, not from a proxy, asks for the portal's plain ("Basic") sign-in and, once a password the
Extension gave has worked, in this browser session or, for the saved password, at any time since it
was saved, names the same sign-in realm as that one; a sign-in in your browser's own box records no
realm, and Forget, Disconnect and a change of portal remove the one on record. It never answers such
a request from one of your browser tabs, which gets your browser's own sign-in box, nor from a
proxy, another website or another realm, nor for the checks that ask whether your portal answers,
which carry no sign-in. Of your own portal tabs' requests it keeps one mark only, and only while an
answer it gave still waits for its outcome: that one of those tabs asked for a sign-in, or had a
request answered by your portal without a refusal, meanwhile (see "One wrong password at most"); it
keeps nothing about the tab or the request itself. It sends your password only to your portal, over
the encrypted connection, as your browser's own box would; never to Licensor or anyone else. It
never writes the password to a log, the audit log or the activity list, and keeps it only as the
next paragraphs say. It never fills in a sign-in web page or your institution's identity provider,
and never types a password into a shell (see the one-time step below). The account name you type is
used only to sign in: the account name of section 1, which the approval check sends, is still the
one your portal's dashboard shows.

One wrong password at most. The Extension sends a password your cluster turns down at most once. The
first refusal stops every sign-in the Extension makes by itself, with a saved password or one you
have just typed, until you type the password again on the sheet, and a saved password your cluster
turned down is not tried again after a restart of the Extension or of your browser; the sheet then
asks you to check the password before you try again. Where the Extension cannot tell whether a
password it gave worked, because your browser closed, or the Extension updated or reloaded, before
your cluster replied, or because one of your portal tabs asked for a sign-in or had a request
answered while that answer waited (you may have signed in in your browser's own box, and your
browser may have used that password for the Extension's request as well), it counts the try as
neither a success nor a refusal and puts a saved password on hold: the Extension does not send it
again until a password you type on the sheet works, or you save one again. Your browser also keeps
its own copy of a password it is given (see below) and sends it again by itself: when your cluster
turns that password down, your browser re-sends its cached copy once for each request already
started with it before the first refusal comes back; the Extension then sends at most one; when the
answered request is abandoned before your cluster replies, your browser may re-send that copy once.
How many of these your cluster counts is not yet measured. Your institution may disable your account
after a few wrong passwords, and getting it back may take days; a password you have just changed may
not work everywhere yet. You accept that risk as a Known Risk (section 8).

Saving the password. Saving it is optional and off unless you tick the box "Remember it on this
computer" on the sheet; the box is never ticked for you. Section 4(a) applies to that choice: where
your institution says to type your password each time you need it, as Wayne State's password
standard does, leave the box unticked. Unticked, the Extension keeps the password only in your
browser's memory, for that one sign-in, and removes it afterwards, whether or not it worked, and at
the latest two minutes after you pressed "Sign in". Ticked, the Extension has your computer make a
passkey for it, unlocked with Windows Hello, Touch ID or your screen lock and named with your
account name followed by "(grid)" (for example, "ab1234 (grid)"), and keeps the password in this
browser profile, on this computer only, encrypted under a key that only that passkey releases. To
use it you unlock it with the passkey, once each time you open your browser, when the Extension next
needs your portal; what the passkey released stays in your browser's memory until the browser closes
or the Extension updates or reloads, and the Extension then signs in with it when your portal asks,
also while none of its pages is open. The passkey is kept by Windows Hello, your Mac's Passwords app
(iCloud Keychain) or Chrome's password manager, not by the Extension; that store may keep it in your
Google account or in iCloud and offer it on your other devices, but the saved password itself stays
only in this browser profile. Someone who can use this browser profile while the saved password is
unlocked, or who can unlock your passkey, could sign in to your portal with it, and so could the
Extension's own code. Where your computer's passkey cannot lock a password, the Extension says so,
uses the password for that one sign-in and from then on offers no box. "Forget", beside "Grid
sign-in" in the Extension's Settings, deletes the saved password and everything the Extension keeps
about it; Disconnect and changing to another portal do the same, and uninstalling the Extension
removes it with the rest of its data. Forget may ask your passkey store to remove the passkey, but
some stores keep it: your list of passkeys may still show it, and you can delete it there, after
Forget. If a password you typed works while your saved one is turned down or on hold, the Extension
forgets the saved one by itself, unless it can tell that the saved one is the same password, which
it then keeps and uses again.

Your browser stays signed in. Once your cluster has accepted a password the Extension gave it, your
browser keeps it in its memory, as it keeps one you type into its own box, and sends it to your
portal from your own portal tabs as well, until the browser closes or you clear its browsing data
with cookies; Forget and Disconnect do not end that.

Your browser's own sign-in box. "Use Chrome's sign-in box instead", on the sheet, opens the portal
in a tab for you to sign in there, in your browser's own sign-in box or on the portal's own sign-in
page; where the Extension cannot answer your portal's request for a sign-in, this is the only way.
That includes a portal that sends you to your institution's own sign-in page instead of asking your
browser for a password: the sheet may still appear there, but the Extension sends no password typed
on it. The Extension never sees what you type in that tab, and closes it once you are signed in,
while it is still on the portal. Your browser may offer to save what you type there; that offer, and
where your browser keeps what it saves, are your browser's own, and section 4(a) applies to them
too. Where your portal does not share its sign-in with the Extension, it keeps that tab behind your
other tabs until the work in hand is done and then closes it, and for a later action opens the
portal in a background tab and closes it again.

The one thing it reads about you from the portal is your account name, from the portal's own
dashboard page. If your portal's shell asks for your cluster password, as it may for an account that
has no key on the cluster, the Extension types nothing there, not even a password you gave it for
the sign-in above, and offers a one-time step instead: it opens the portal's own terminal page in a
tab, and you type your password there yourself; the Extension never sees it. Once you say you are
in, or the page's title shows that your shell is up, the Extension types one line into that terminal
(or leaves it on your clipboard for you to paste), which runs the Framework's login-setup step in
your own account. That step makes a key in your cluster account, in its ~/.ssh folder, that opens
your account only from the computer the terminal came from, which at your institution is its Open
OnDemand server (it is a file in your own account, protected as your other files are, and hidden
from Claude Code inside your worker; anyone who obtained it and could connect from that server could
use it), and has ssh offer it when the portal's shell connects you, so that the shell need not ask
for your password; it never reads, changes or prints a key you already have. The key stays in your
cluster account: the Extension never sees it, keeps nothing about it in your browser and sends
nothing about it to Licensor. The command "hpcworker login-setup --remove", run in a terminal on the
cluster, removes it. This step has been tried only against the Licensor's mock portal, not yet on a
real cluster by an account without a key.

(ii) What an earlier version may have left. If you used the direct way of versions 12.0.0 to 15.0.0
of the Extension on this computer, three things of it may remain, and this version uses none of
them. The Bridge stays installed on your computer until you remove it: on Windows, from Windows' own
list of installed apps, where it is called "Beethoven bridge"; on macOS and Linux, and for a Bridge
installed from a zip file with Beethoven 14.0.0 or earlier, with the uninstaller placed beside it.
This version holds no permission to start it, and your browser starts it for nothing. The line that
version added to your account's ~/.ssh/authorized_keys file on your cluster stays until it is
removed. This version deletes the key that line admitted from your browser, unread, on its first run
after the update, so the line now admits nothing; it never let that key run anything but the
Framework's gate, a fixed list of short commands, and this version never uses the line and never
types a command for it. Your lab lead can remove the line for you, and the command "hpcworker
browser-key remove", run in a terminal on your cluster, removes it; withdrawing your approval under
section 3 does not. Where that version recorded that it had added such a line, the Extension's
Settings say so once, with the date, and offer "Forget", which removes only that note from your
browser and changes nothing on your cluster. A passkey you made to protect that key, the old passkey
of versions 12.0.0 to 15.0.0, is kept by your system or your browser's password manager, not by the
Extension; it is named with your account name alone, or "beethoven", this version never uses it,
and you can delete it there. It is not the passkey of (i), named with your account name followed by
"(grid)", which this version uses for a saved password: delete that one only after Forget.

(c) Your account name, as your cluster confirmed it, and the answer of the last approval check with
its time are stored in the Extension's own storage in your browser. What the sign-in of (b)(i) keeps
in your browser, and for how long, is listed in PRIVACY.md. Nothing about your approval is copied to
your cluster account; the Framework has no key, password or account list of Licensor's.

(d) The Framework makes a random token for each job so that only your own browser session can reach
your worker. It is kept in a file only you can read, is never shown, and never appears on a command
line or in a job log.

(e) Where your work goes. Files the worker reads and every command's output are sent to Anthropic.
While you are connected through claude.ai the transcript is also stored on Anthropic's servers. A
personal plan is retained five years if model training is on and 30 days otherwise. No BAA covers a
personal plan. FERPA records, IRB data and export-controlled technical data must not be in the
working directory. Remote Control usage draws from your plan's shared limit. The Extension shows
these sentences in short form, with the full text one click away, before your first worker starts,
and a one-line reminder beside Connect each time you start one, and does not start a worker in a
folder you have marked restricted.

(f) The Extension runs no code on claude.ai or any other Anthropic page and reads nothing from them.
Its only page script runs on the one portal website you connect: where your portal does not share
its sign-in with the Extension, to carry the Extension's requests there, and, during the one-time
step in (b)(i), on the portal's terminal page, where it watches only the page's title and types only
the one line described there. It opens an Anthropic page only in a new tab, as a bookmark would: the
sign-in page during the sign-in described in (a), your worker's session page, or the page of a
project of yours on claude.ai whose address you have given in the Extension's settings, when a
worker you started is ready (unless you turn that off) and when you choose "Open my session", "Open
my project" or "New session", the connectors settings page if you choose the optional Google Drive
step, and the plans page if you follow "See plans" on the welcome page; it closes the sign-in tab it
opened once you are signed in, and it may bring forward a claude.ai tab it opened itself. Of an
Anthropic tab it opened, it learns only that the tab has begun loading a page, which the browser
tells it without the page's address or content. In the same way, the welcome page and the step that
asks you to turn on your VPN link your institution's own pages about its computing accounts and its
VPN; each opens in a new tab only when you choose it, and the Extension learns nothing of your
visit.

(g) Of (a) to (f), only your account name in (c) is sent to Licensor, in the approval check that
section 10 describes; nothing else is, and in particular not your cluster password or the account
name you type to sign in. Section 10 describes the only things that may be.

(h) For a lab lead. Settings' folded "For your lab lead" offers "Connect GitHub Actions" and
"Remove". Only if you press one does the Extension type one line into your portal's shell, which
runs the Framework's gate setup, or its removal, from your own copy of the Framework in your
computing account, for the one GitHub repository you name. The setup, with the GitHub sign-in
already in your computing account, makes a key, lets it only start, check or stop that
repository's runner jobs in your account, stores it as a secret of that repository on GitHub, and
starts the repository's gate workflow; the removal takes the key's line out of your account. You are
responsible for the repository and for what its workflows run in your account under section 4. The
Extension never sees the key or your GitHub sign-in, stores nothing of it, and sends nothing of it to
Licensor.
